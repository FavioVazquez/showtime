# Why the sea has tides (source notes)

Use only these facts. They are standard, textbook physics.

1. Tides are the regular rise and fall of sea level, caused mainly by the Moon's gravity, with a
   smaller contribution from the Sun.
2. Gravity weakens with distance, so the Moon pulls harder on the side of Earth facing it than on
   Earth's centre, and harder on Earth's centre than on the far side. This difference in pull is
   the tidal force.
3. The tidal force stretches the oceans into two bulges: one facing the Moon and one on the
   opposite side of Earth.
4. As Earth rotates, a coastline passes through both bulges, so most places get two high tides and
   two low tides a day.
5. The Moon moves along its orbit while Earth turns, so the cycle takes about 24 hours and 50
   minutes; high tide arrives roughly 50 minutes later each day.
6. The Sun's tidal effect is a little under half of the Moon's.
7. When the Sun, Moon and Earth line up (new moon and full moon), the effects add up: larger
   "spring tides". When they are at right angles (first and last quarter), they partly cancel:
   smaller "neap tides". Spring tides have nothing to do with the season.
8. Coastline shape and sea-floor depth change how big tides are locally; some bays have a tidal
   range of many metres, some seas have almost none.
